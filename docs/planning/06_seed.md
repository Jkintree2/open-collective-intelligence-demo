**Enforcement of international law**

S4. Drafted (Seed): "The International Court of Justice can rule on disputes between states, but its judgments are only enforced if the Security Council chooses to act. A court whose rulings depend on the goodwill of the parties is not a court in the ordinary sense."
Claims: Enforcement of international law. Proposes and approves: Compulsory jurisdiction of the International Court of Justice. Cites: UN Charter, Article 94 (https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/chapter-14), supporting the claim.

S5. Drafted (Seed): "Most states have never accepted the International Court of Justice's compulsory jurisdiction, and several that once did have since withdrawn. International law binds only those who agree to be bound."
Claims: Enforcement of international law. Cites: International Court of Justice, declarations recognizing the jurisdiction of the Court as compulsory (https://www.icj-cij.org/declarations), supporting the claim.

S6. Counterpoint: "Global rules can be enforced without a world government. The Montreal Protocol phased out ozone depleting chemicals through trade measures and a funding mechanism, and nearly every country complied."
Claims: Enforcement of international law. Proposes: Treaty based enforcement with trade measures (no position recorded). **Security Council veto**

S7. Drafted (Seed): "Any one of the five permanent members can block a Security Council resolution, whatever the rest of the world thinks. Since 1946 the veto has been used hundreds of times, often to shield the member casting it or its allies."
Claims: Security Council veto. Proposes and approves: Limit or abolish the Security Council veto. Cites: UN Charter, Article 27 (https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/chapter-5) and the Dag Hammarskjöld Library's list of Security Council vetoes (https://research.un.org/en/docs/sc/quick), both supporting the claim.

S8. Counterpoint: "The General Assembly can act when the Council is blocked. The Uniting for Peace resolution of 1950 lets the Assembly recommend collective measures, and since 2022 the Assembly meets within ten days of any veto to debate it."
Claims: Security Council veto. Cites: Uniting for Peace, General Assembly resolution 377 (V) (https://research.un.org/en/docs/ga/quick/emergency) and General Assembly resolution 76/262 of 2022, the veto initiative (https://docs.un.org/en/A/RES/76/262), both refuting the claim that the veto is absolute.

**Global climate coordination**

S11. Drafted (Seed): "Carbon pricing now exists in dozens of countries, but it covers only about a quarter of global emissions and most prices are far below what would be needed to meet the Paris goals. A single global price would remove the incentive to move pollution across borders."

## Technical notes (not client-facing)

**File.** `seed/seed.json`. Every seed statement is a post in exactly the card payload shape from `05_extraction.md`, plus `id`, `author` and `created_at`. The seed script calls the same `merge_post()` function the app uses, with `seed: true` and `source: "seed"`. There is no second write path to keep in sync.

    {"name": "Need for world government", "parent": null},

      "id": "seed-01",
      "author": "John Kintree",
      "created_at": "2026-09-01T10:00:00Z",
      "text": "Our best protection from all of those risks might be …",
      "issues": [{"name": "Need for world government", "parent": null}],

        {"name": "Universal Declaration of Human Rights", "url": "https://www.ohchr.org/en/human-rights/universal-declaration/translations/english", "stance": "supports", "about": "World Constitution based on the UDHR and Earth Charter"},

**Issues block first.** The six issues are merged before the posts so `PART_OF` edges exist even if John's approved text moves a statement between parts. The parent issue gets its `PART_OF` children from this block, not from posts.

**Persons.** Three: `name:john kintree` ("John Kintree") for S1, S2, S3, S9 and S14; `name:seed` ("Seed") for the seven drafted statements; `name:counterpoint` ("Counterpoint", or the name John picks) for S6 and S8. When John approves a drafted statement, its `author` field changes to "John Kintree"; "Reload seed" alone is not enough, because the CLAIM and APPROVE edges already exist from Seed, so run "Reset to seed" if no tester has posted yet, otherwise delete that seed post from the admin page and reload. If John types "John Kintree" on the live site, his posts land on the same Person as his seed claims, which is what he wants.

**Timestamps.** Spread over 1 to 4 September 2026 in the file, so "Most recent" has an order on day one and seed posts sit below anything a tester writes. `merge_post()` accepts an optional `created_at` for seed use only. Under "Most people", Enforcement of international law and Global climate coordination tie (2 people, 3 claims each); give S6 the latest date of those two families so Enforcement lists first, which is what Session 2's checklist expects.

**Counts after load.** 6 Issue, 3 Person, 14 Post, 9 Solution, 14 Evidence. Edges: 14 POSTED, 14 CLAIM, 11 PROPOSE, 9 HAVE_PROPOSED (the carbon tax pair from S9 and S11 merges to one; S14's pair already exists from S3), 9 APPROVE (one per person and solution; S14 merges onto John's existing approval from S3; S9 is John's and S11 is Seed's until John approves S11, after which it becomes 8), 16 SUBMIT, 13 SUPPORTS, 3 REFUTES, 5 PART_OF. **Links.** Each URL was chosen for stability (UN Charter chapter pages, ohchr.org, earthcharter.org, unep.org, the World Bank dashboard, icj-cij.org, ozone.unep.org, research.un.org library guides, docs.un.org resolver, unpacampaign.org, e-estonia.com).
